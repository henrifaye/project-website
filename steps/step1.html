Basilica di San Petronio

First, we created a query that would allow us to find what entities related to the church of San Petronio already existed on ArCo.

PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>
PREFIX arco: <https://w3id.org/arco/ontology/arco/>

SELECT DISTINCT ?site ?label
WHERE {
   { ?site rdfs:label ?label.
   FILTER(REGEX(?label, "chiesa di san petronio", "i")) 
   }
UNION
   { ?site rdfs:label ?label.
   FILTER(REGEX(?label, "basilica di san petronio", "i"))            
   }
}
ORDER BY(?site)

In the results above, we found two entities labeled as "Chiesa di San Petronio", both of them referring to the same church in Bologna. It was interesting to notice, though, that one of them had only little information, while the other one contained many more properties.

We therefore decided to add missing information that we deemed relevant to the second entity and then link the two resources together.

In order to do so, we first executed a second query to make sure that the two pieces of information we wanted to add, a description and the commissioner, didn't already exist somewhere else without being connected to the church.

PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>
PREFIX core: <https://w3id.org/arco/ontology/core/>
PREFIX a-cd: <https://w3id.org/arco/ontology/contextdescription/>

SELECT DISTINCT ?site ?label ?value
WHERE {
   ?site rdfs:label ?label.
   FILTER(REGEX(?label, "chiesa di san petronio", "i")) 
   OPTIONAL {
   ?site core:description ?value .
   }
   OPTIONAL {
   ?site a-cd:hasCommittent ?value.
   }
}

The results showed that neither of these properties was already present, so we proceeded with our enrichment.


LLMs Prompting and Comparison

For this part of the project, we decided to compare mostly ChatGPT and Gemini. We used the zero-shot and few-shot prompting technicques to gather the missing information related to the Basilica di San Petronio, which we later verified through the official website of the church and Museoitalia, an Italian website containing up-to-date information about Italy's artistic and cultural heritage.

The third prompting technique, chain-of-thought prompting, has been used later in the project to create new RDF triples.

Zero-shot prompting

We used the same prompt for both ChatGPT and Gemini, and the difference in the output was immediately visibile.

First, we used ChatGPT. This LLM usedSan Petronio's official website and Museoitalia as sources. Furthermore, it only provided measurements in the metrical system and its style was overall simpler and less formal.

Gemini used instead Wikipedia and the travel website myCityHunt as source. Other than the measurements in both meters and feet, it also provided an image of the façade and the height of the bell tower. It mentioned the Cassini Meridian Line inside the church as well.

Our second prompt regarded a textual description of the church:

Give me a short description of around 10 lines of the Basilica di San Petronio

This time, the outputs were rather similar.

ChatGPT: The Basilica di San Petronio is one of the most important Gothic churches in Bologna. It stands prominently in Piazza Maggiore, at the heart of the historic city centre. The basilica is dedicated to Saint Petronius, the patron saint and former bishop of Bologna. Construction began in 1390, following a decision by the city's medieval government. It was designed by the architect Antonio di Vincenzo and was conceived as a civic monument. The building is remarkable for its enormous dimensions, measuring about 132 metres long and 60 metres wide. Its unfinished façade combines white marble with red brick and features elaborate Gothic decoration. The interior contains a vast nave, side chapels, monumental artworks, and a famous meridian line. The original project was even more ambitious, but financial and political difficulties prevented its completion. Today, San Petronio remains a major symbol of Bologna's religious heritage and civic identity.
Gemini: Dominating Bologna's main square, Piazza Maggiore, the Basilica di San Petronio is one of the world's largest Gothic structures and the biggest brick church ever built. Construction began in 1390 under architect Antonio di Vincenzo as a grand monument to civic pride, commissioned by the city's commune rather than the Papacy. Its iconic exterior is instantly recognizable for its half-finished facade—marbled at the lower base by Jacopo della Quercia and raw, exposed brown brick above, after funding halted and papal intervention blocked plans to make it larger than St. Peter's. Inside, the vast three-aisled interior features soaring stone vaulting, 22 rich side chapels holding Renaissance masterpieces like Giovanni da Modena's frescoes, and the famous 67-meter Cassini Meridian Line drawn into the floor in 1655, which still functions as a precise astronomical calendar today.
